Methods, assumptions and sources

Wall contact and free segment

The adapted profile follows r = rA(z) from the rim to a departure point between the equator (or a lower rim) and the neck. A quadratic Bézier cap then reaches the proximal pole. Its initial tangent matches the wall and its pole tangent is horizontal: position and tangent continuity (G1), not curvature continuity.

P₀ = (rA(zd), zd)
P₁ = (rA(zd) − a rA′(zd), zd − a)
P₂ = (0, zd − a)
B(t) = (1−t)²P₀ + 2t(1−t)P₁ + t²P₂
Pole curvature radius ρ = 2P₁ᵣ²/a

A horizontal tangent alone can still look pointed near the pole. A minimum pole curvature radius (default 0.10 mm, adjustable) excludes sharply curved caps. This is a synthetic geometric bound, not a force or material property.

The same reference wire budget L₀ is retained. In the adapted model, total twist 2πn is uniform per meridional arc length s: dφ/ds = 2πn/S. Both segments are integrated to solve cap depth a. This differs from the original ellipse’s uniform parameter-angle law.

The search tests 61 departure heights and 16 depth intervals, choosing the first admissible result. It requires a narrowing free cap and checks wall penetration at 601 points per segment (10⁻⁶ mm tolerance). Minimum wall descent is a lower bound on vertical contact distance, not adhesion. Search failure keeps an explicitly labelled reference ellipse visible; it is not an adapted contact solution. Below the neck, no arterial wall is invented.

Reference ellipse

r(u) = R sin(πu/2)
z(u) = zₚ + h [1 − cos(πu/2)]
φ(u) = 2πnu
L = ∫₀¹ √[r′² + z′² + r²φ′²] du

The reference wire length uses R₀ = D₀/2 and h₀ = qD₀. Defaults q = 0.35 and n = 0.5 are arbitrary and uncalibrated. At a prescribed aperture, depth is solved by bisection. If the sac is wider than the device, a nominal-width free cup is shown, centred at the selected rim height. Dashed horizontal lines measure the rim-to-wall gap on each side. This placement is illustrative, not a predicted equilibrium. Any lower wall intersections remain highlighted. Devices entirely inside the sac remain visible.

The sac combines a smooth cubic segment below the equator with a quarter ellipse above it. Profiles are never clipped against the wall to make them fit.

Scope and sources

The model assumes axial symmetry, a rigid wall and a prescribed twist distribution. It does not solve friction, shape memory, forces, release mechanics, two layers, porosity or haemodynamics. Radial gaps and neck-plane crossing do not establish neck coverage or clinical stability.
